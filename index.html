The craft of a public voice

How a speech
brings people together.

To study how the Prime Minister of India gives a public speech, listen for a clear idea, inclusive language, purposeful repetition, and a practical call to action. These tools help a speaker connect a shared vision to everyday life.

01

Begin with what matters

Connect an ambitious idea to everyday hopes, honest challenges, and the lives people recognize.

02

Make the message memorable

Use clear language, purposeful repetition, and a vivid image so the central idea travels beyond the stage.

03

Turn emotion into action

Pair hope with something practical: a choice, a habit, a promise, or a first step everyone can take.

These are general public-speaking principles, not an imitation of any person's voice. The speeches below are original writing—not quotations, transcripts, or official remarks by the Prime Minister of India.

A brief journey through history

India's Prime Ministers,
from Nehru onward.

Meet the leaders who have served as Prime Minister since independence. Each short profile highlights a chapter in India's changing story.

01

1947–1964

Jawaharlal Nehru

When India became independent in 1947, Jawaharlal Nehru became its first Prime Minister and helped guide a new nation through its earliest years. He worked to establish democratic institutions and championed science, education, and large development projects. His long tenure helped set the direction of the young republic.

02

1964 · 1966 · Acting

Gulzarilal Nanda

Gulzarilal Nanda was a labour economist who had spent years in public service before becoming Prime Minister. Twice, after the deaths of Nehru and Shastri, he stepped in as acting Prime Minister while the country prepared for its next leader. Though each spell was brief, he helped provide continuity at two difficult turning points.

03

1964–1966

Lal Bahadur Shastri

Lal Bahadur Shastri rose from a modest background to lead India after Nehru. During the 1965 war, he rallied the nation with “Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan”—a call to honour both soldiers and farmers. He died in Tashkent in 1966, shortly after signing a peace agreement.

04

1966–1977 · 1980–1984

Indira Gandhi

Indira Gandhi became India's first woman Prime Minister in 1966. Her years in office included the 1971 war and the Emergency, a deeply controversial period from 1975 to 1977; after losing the 1977 election, she returned to power in 1980. Her life and political journey ended with her assassination in 1984.

05

1977–1979

Morarji Desai

Morarji Desai became Prime Minister in 1977, after voters rejected the government that had imposed the Emergency. Leading the Janata Party, he became India's first Prime Minister from outside Congress. His government marked a historic change of power through the ballot box.

06

1979–1980

Charan Singh

Charan Singh built his public life around the concerns of farmers and rural communities. In 1979, shifting alliances brought him to the office of Prime Minister, but his government fell before it could face a confidence vote in Parliament. His short tenure reflected the uncertainty of India's coalition politics at the time.

07

1984–1989

Rajiv Gandhi

Rajiv Gandhi entered national leadership after the assassination of his mother, Indira Gandhi, in 1984. During his time as Prime Minister, his government encouraged the growth of computing and telecommunications. The country was beginning to connect with technologies that would transform work and communication in the years ahead.

08

1989–1990

Vishwanath Pratap Singh

Vishwanath Pratap Singh became Prime Minister in 1989 at the head of a coalition government. One of the defining decisions of his tenure was implementing the Mandal Commission recommendations on reservations for Other Backward Classes in central government jobs. The decision brought questions of representation and social justice to the centre of national debate.

09

1990–1991

Chandra Shekhar

Chandra Shekhar became Prime Minister in 1990, leading a minority government during a difficult period for India's politics and economy. His administration lasted only a few months amid shifting parliamentary support. He resigned in 1991, and the country prepared for another election.

10

1991–1996

P. V. Narasimha Rao

P. V. Narasimha Rao took office in 1991 as India faced a severe balance-of-payments crisis. Working with Finance Minister Manmohan Singh, his government launched major economic reforms that changed how India engaged with the world economy. Rao's tenure opened a consequential new chapter in the country's economic history.

11

1996 · 1998–2004

Atal Bihari Vajpayee

Atal Bihari Vajpayee brought a poet's voice and a parliamentarian's experience to national leadership. His first government lasted just 13 days in 1996; he later returned to lead a coalition government that completed a full term. His years in office included the 1998 nuclear tests and the launch of major highway development.

12

1996–1997

H. D. Deve Gowda

H. D. Deve Gowda had built his political career in Karnataka before becoming Prime Minister in 1996. He led the United Front, a coalition that brought regional parties into national government. His rise illustrated how India's political story was increasingly shaped by voices from across its states.

13

1997–1998

I. K. Gujral

I. K. Gujral brought long experience in diplomacy to the office of Prime Minister in 1997. He is remembered for the Gujral Doctrine, which called for trust and cooperation with India's smaller neighbours. His brief tenure kept regional relationships at the heart of the national conversation.

14

2004–2014

Manmohan Singh

Manmohan Singh was an economist who had helped lead India's 1991 economic reforms as Finance Minister. He became Prime Minister in 2004 and served for a decade. During those years, Parliament passed the Right to Information Act, and India reached a landmark civil nuclear agreement with the United States.

15

2014–present

Narendra Modi

Narendra Modi became Prime Minister in 2014 after serving as Chief Minister of Gujarat. His governments have promoted programmes including Digital India, Swachh Bharat Mission, and financial inclusion. After winning a third consecutive term, he began serving again in 2024.

Gulzarilal Nanda served as acting Prime Minister during two transitions and is included here in the historical sequence. Dates indicate terms in office.
